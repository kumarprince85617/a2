Archival Knowledge

Technical & Collector Reference

Comprehensive answers concerning our small-batch copper kettle preserving methods, ingredient origins, and proper storage protocols.

Why does copper cauldron manufacturing matter for apricot confitures?
Copper is the premier thermal conductor in traditional culinary science. When copper heats, thermal energy disperses evenly throughout the pot walls, preventing hot spots that scorch delicate sugars. This enables gentle moisture evaporation at lower temperatures, preserving fruit terpenes, natural beta-carotene colors, and vibrant tartness.
What is the exact shelf life and preservation protocol for unopened jars?
Unopened jars held in cool, dark storage conditions between 50°F and 65°F will maintain peak aromatics for up to twenty-four months from the date of bottling. Because we use natural sugar-moisture equilibrium rather than synthetic chemical preservatives, avoid exposing jars to high humidity or direct solar radiation.
How should an opened jar of Apricot Velvet be stored?
Once the hermetic vacuum seal is broken, keep the jar refrigerated between 35°F and 40°F and consume within six to eight weeks. Always use a clean, dry spoon to remove preserves, as residual moisture or bread crumbs will introduce unwanted microbial contamination.
Do you ship international orders to culinary collectors outside North America?
Yes. We coordinate temperature-monitored international air freight for private collectors and pastry establishments throughout Europe, Asia, and the Middle East. Contact our Chicago atelier at +1-877-667-7705 or via our tasting salon desk to arrange custom customs documentation and expedited transport.
